West Norristown is the part of the borough that people usually call the West End. It is a residential area of attached houses, and the National Register of Historic Places lists a large part of it as the West Norristown Historic District. This page is for homeowners and landlords there who want a plumber to call about a drain, a leak or a water heater.

If you want the borough as a whole, start at our Norristown plumbing page. If your block is nearer the center of town, see Central Norristown.

Why it matters for plumbing

Attached houses share walls, so a leak or a backup can reach a neighbor. That is general information, not a finding about any one street.

Plumbing in attached houses

A rowhouse or a twin shares at least one wall with the house next door. That changes how a plumbing problem plays out, because water that escapes in one house can show up as a stain or a damp wall in the next one. It also means a conversation with the neighbor is sometimes part of the fix.

We cannot tell you what is inside the walls of a particular West Norristown house without looking. Pipe material, drain material and the age of fixtures vary from house to house, and many have been updated over the years. A visit and a look at your shutoff valves, your drain cleanout and your water heater tells us far more than the age of the street.

Drain trouble is a good example. A slow kitchen sink and a gurgling basement floor drain can have different causes, and we start with drain cleaning when the problem looks like a blockage. If a clog keeps returning in a main line, a camera look through sewer inspection shows what is in the pipe before anyone digs.

What the historic district listing does and does not mean

The West Norristown Historic District covers about 220 acres and has roughly 1,700 buildings. The National Register describes it as roughly bounded by Stoney Creek and by Selma and Elm Streets, and it was added to the register on November 23, 1984. Source: the National Register of Historic Places listing for the district.

Plumbing work inside a house is usually a different matter from the outside of the building. According to the National Park Service, National Register listing places no restrictions on what a private owner does with private funds, though state or local preservation laws may apply. We do not know whether a given block carries a local rule. Ask the borough before you plan work that changes the outside of a house, and ask us about the plumbing.

Services West Norristown homes call about

Most calls from attached houses fall into a few groups. Backups and slow drains lead to drain cleaning and, for the main line, sewer line cleaning. A damp spot on a shared wall points to leak detection, which finds the source before a wall is opened.

Water heaters in a basement are another regular call. If yours leaks or no longer keeps up, our water heater replacement page explains what we look at. For a pipe that has already burst, see burst pipe repair.

Before you call about a leak or a backup

These steps help in any house, and they matter more when a wall is shared.

Find the main shutoff

Turn it clockwise to stop the water. In an attached house the valve is often in the basement near the front wall. If it will not turn, do not force it.

Stop using the drains

If a floor drain or a toilet is backing up, stop running water in the house until we talk.

Check the neighbor wall

Look for damp plaster or a stain on the shared wall. Tell us what you see, and tell your neighbor too.
