Who we are

Norristown Express Plumbing is a plumbing service for Norristown, Pennsylvania. We take emergency calls at any hour and schedule planned work during the day for homes and small businesses in Norristown and the nearby Montgomery County towns.

This site explains our services, the areas we serve and the plain steps to take when something goes wrong. We keep the claims on it limited to what we can stand behind.

How we work

When you call, we ask where the water is and what you can reach safely. We tell you what to shut off, send a plumber and give you a clear price before work starts. After the repair, we explain what failed and what to do so it does not return.

If a job needs a specialist, such as an electrician for new wiring or a heating contractor for combustion work, we say so instead of guessing.

What we do not say

You will not find review counts, star ratings or years in business on this site, because we do not display claims that you cannot check. You can check a Pennsylvania home improvement contractor registration at hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov, and we encourage you to check anyone you hire.

Emergency and planned work

Emergency calls are the ones where water is moving or backing up, a heater has failed, or a pipe has split. We treat those first and keep the line open at any hour. Planned work includes water heater installs, repiping, fixture installs and basement drainage, and we schedule it so you can plan around it.

Both kinds of work get the same treatment on the phone. You hear what we think is wrong, what it may involve and what to expect on price, and you decide whether to go ahead.

Our service area

We work in Norristown and the nearby Montgomery County towns of Bridgeport, King of Prussia, Blue Bell, Collegeville and Eagleville. Each town has its own page with local plumbing notes and the ZIP codes we cover there.

Why the local notes matter

Plumbing problems depend on the age of the house, the kind of pipe in the ground and who runs the public water and sewer. We write those local details into our pages so that you can tell quickly whether a problem is yours to fix or belongs to the utility. Norristown, for example, gets water from Pennsylvania American Water and sewer service from the Norristown Municipal Waste Authority.

Licensing and registration

Pennsylvania does not issue a statewide plumbing license, and some municipalities have their own rules. Contractors who do at least five thousand dollars a year in home improvement work must register with the Pennsylvania Attorney General. You can look up registrations at hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov. Registration is not an endorsement, but it is a useful check on anyone who works on your home.

What a first call sounds like

A typical call starts with three questions. Where is the water, what can you reach safely, and how long has it been happening. The answers tell us whether to send a crew with a drain machine, a pipe repair kit or a replacement heater, and what to ask you to shut off before we arrive.

If the problem is in the street, such as a water main break or a public sewer blockage, we tell you who to call, because paying for a visit that cannot fix the problem helps no one.

Questions we hear from callers

People often ask whether we charge a different rate after midnight. After hours visits can carry a different charge, and we tell you on the phone before we send anyone. They also ask whether we work on rentals. We do, and we can talk to a landlord or property manager if you prefer.

A third common question is whether we can fix something we did not install. Yes. Most of the plumbing in the area was put in long before any of us arrived, and part of the job is working out what is there and how best to repair it.

What helps us when you call

A few details save time on the phone. Have the street address ready, along with the kind of fixture involved and whether the water is clear or dirty. If you can reach the shutoff valve under the fixture or at the water meter, tell us, and we walk you through closing it.

Photos are welcome by email afterward, but they are never a reason to wait. For anything that is actively leaking or backing up, the phone is always the quickest route, and a plumber can start planning the job while you are still talking.
