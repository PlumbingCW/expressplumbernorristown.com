A plumbing failure in a commercial building affects more than a bathroom. A kitchen that cannot drain cannot serve. A burst line over a retail floor can close the space. A multi unit building has tenants calling at all hours.

We handle commercial plumbing emergencies and the maintenance that prevents them, and we coordinate with property managers who need clear communication.

The calls we see most in commercial buildings

Kitchen drains and grease lines top the list for restaurants. Floor drains that back up, grease interceptors that overflow, and prep sinks that drain slowly all trace to the same cause, which is fat and food solids settling in the line.

In offices and multi unit buildings the common calls are clogged toilets in shared restrooms, leaking supply lines above a ceiling, and water heaters that fail across several units. These can involve more than one tenant, so we keep the property manager informed.

Grease, floor drains and interceptors

Grease interceptors and traps are designed to catch fat before it reaches the sewer. They only work if they are cleaned on schedule. A full interceptor lets grease pass downstream and clog the line.

We clear the lines connected to the kitchen and recommend a schedule. For lines that keep clogging we use hydro jetting to clean the pipe wall. Interceptor pumping is a separate service that a licensed hauler handles.

Business plumbing in Norristown

Norristown is the county seat, with courthouse offices, small businesses along DeKalb Street and Main Street, the Montgomery County Cultural Center and Arts Hill, and many older mixed use buildings. Lines in older commercial buildings may be shared between neighbors.

The Norristown Municipal Waste Authority runs the public sewer and has requirements for what goes into it. If you run a restaurant, ask the authority about grease rules. We help you understand what the work you need involves.

Model plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) call for grease interceptors where commercial kitchens discharge fats and oils. The Norristown Municipal Waste Authority can tell you what its own requirements are for food service.
