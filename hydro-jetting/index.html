Hydro jetting sends water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that pushes water forward and backward inside the pipe. The water scours grease, scale and soft root growth from the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through it.

It is the right tool for a line that clogs again soon after a cable cleaning, for restaurant kitchens that build grease, and for long runs where buildup has narrowed the pipe over many years.

Jetting compared with cabling

A cable is quick, inexpensive and good for a fresh clog. It leaves the pipe wall mostly as it found it. Jetting removes the film and scale that a cable skips, which is why a jetted line stays clear longer.

Jetting is not right for every pipe. A cracked, offset or badly corroded line can be damaged by pressure, so we look inside with a camera first when the pipe condition is unknown. If the pipe is failing, cleaning it harder is the wrong answer.

How a jetting visit goes

We start at the cleanout and send a camera down if the pipe condition is unknown. We then run the jetter in steps, working the nozzle forward through the line and drawing it back so that debris moves toward the cleanout.

The line is run with water again afterward and rechecked with the camera when the history calls for it. You see what came out, and you get a plain statement about whether the pipe wall looks sound.

Lines that tend to need jetting

Kitchen lines with grease

A line that carries years of fat and soap builds a hard layer a cable only breaks through.

Mineral scale in older pipe

Rough scale narrows the pipe and catches paper and hair.

Soft root growth

Fine roots can be flushed out at the joint after a cutter opens the way.

Long horizontal runs

Low slope and distance let solids settle, and jetting moves them along.

Where jetting comes up in Norristown

The borough has a dense mix of rowhouses, small commercial buildings along Main Street and DeKalb Street, and restaurants that share old lines with neighbors. Grease in shared and older lines is a common reason a restaurant or a multi unit building needs more than a cable.

We also see homes where a basement line was clear for years and then began to clog when a neighboring tree grew. If roots are the cause, jetting alone will not stop them from returning and the joint may need a repair.

Grease is a leading cause of sewer blockages, according to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. Commercial kitchens are often expected to follow grease management guidelines set by the local sewer authority, so ask the Norristown Municipal Waste Authority what applies to you.
