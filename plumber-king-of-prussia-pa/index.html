King of Prussia is not a borough or a city. It is a census designated place inside Upper Merion Township, best known for the King of Prussia Mall and for sitting beside Valley Forge National Historical Park. The name comes from the King of Prussia Inn, which dates to 1719.

We reach it from Norristown along US 202 and the surrounding highways, and we answer after hours calls for homes, condos, offices and shops across the 19406 ZIP code.

A mix of homes and businesses

Suburban development in King of Prussia took off from the 1960s, so much of its housing is mid century and newer. That means fewer galvanized lines than the older boroughs, but plenty of copper that is decades old and water heaters that have reached the end of a typical life.

The area also has a large commercial base, with tens of thousands of people working here, which means many calls come from offices, retail and apartment complexes. We handle those through our commercial plumbing service.

Hot water in suburban homes

Many King of Prussia homes have a tank water heater in a utility closet, garage or basement. A leaking tank in a closet runs under the wall and into carpet, so a quick shutoff matters. Know where your cold water valve is.

If your heater is old and you have time to plan, a scheduled swap beats a flooded closet. See our water heater installation page for how that goes.

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, King of Prussia is a census designated place and not an incorporated municipality, which is why it sits inside Upper Merion Township for permits and local rules. Ask the township which permits apply to your project.

Condos, townhomes and shared shutoffs

In a condo or townhome, a leak can come from your unit or from the unit above. Before you call, check whether neighbors see water too. If the leak is from a shared line, the property manager may need to shut off a building valve.

Keep your unit shutoff location written down and tell guests and tenants. A shutoff that is easy to find turns a flood into a cleanup.
