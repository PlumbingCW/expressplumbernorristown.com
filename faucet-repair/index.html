A dripping faucet is an annoyance. A faucet that will not shut off, a handle that has broken, or a line under the sink that is spraying is an emergency. The fix is usually fast once the water is off.

We repair faucets in kitchens, bathrooms and showers and we replace the shutoff valves that let you control the water to them.

Stop the water first

Look under the sink for two small valves, one for hot and one for cold. Turn each clockwise until it stops. If they will not move, use the main shutoff. A shutoff that will not turn is itself a repair we do on the spot.

For a tub or shower valve with no shutoff, the main is your control. This is another reason we recommend adding a local shutoff to any fixture that lacks one.

Signs to repair now

Water behind or under the sink

A leaking supply line or drain can ruin a cabinet in days.

Handle spins or will not hold

A stripped screw or broken stem needs replacement.

Spray pattern is off

Mineral buildup in the aerator is common. A quick clean often fixes it.

Drip that does not stop

A constant drip wastes water and wears the seat.

Repair or replace a faucet

Many faucets can be repaired with a new cartridge or seals, and we keep common parts on the truck. Older or discontinued models may not have parts, and then a replacement faucet is the better path.

We install faucets you supply or help you choose. If you are also working on the toilet or disposal in the same room, see our toilet repair and garbage disposal pages.

Faucets and water in the Norristown area

Mineral deposits in aerators and cartridges can cause weak flow and drips. If you suspect your water is the cause, read the annual water quality report from Pennsylvania American Water at pennsylvaniaamwater.com/wqreports.

In older Norristown homes the shutoff valves under sinks may be decades old and stuck. We replace them when we are there, because a good shutoff turns the next emergency into a minor job.

According to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's WaterSense program, labeled bathroom sink faucets flow at no more than 1.5 gallons per minute. A new aerator or faucet with that label is an easy way to cut water use.

Aerators, cartridges and mineral buildup

Low flow and spray that goes sideways usually come from mineral buildup in the aerator, the small screen at the tip. Unscrew it, soak it in vinegar and rinse, and flow often returns. If the problem is at the cartridge, mineral buildup can also make a handle stiff or gritty.

If you flush out a cartridge and the handle is still rough, the cartridge is worn. Replacing it costs less than a new faucet and restores smooth operation.

When a shutoff valve will not close

A shutoff that will not close fully is a risk, because it leaves you with no way to stop water at the fixture. Old valves can seize or crumble when turned. We replace them with quarter turn valves that are easier to operate and last longer.

If you find a stuck valve, do not force it. A cracked valve stem can start a leak you cannot stop. Call and we handle it with the water off at the main.
