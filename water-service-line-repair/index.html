The water service line is the buried pipe that connects the public water main to your house. When it leaks, you may lose pressure, see a wet patch in the yard, or notice that the meter keeps running with everything off.

We find the leak, explain whether it is on your side or the utility's, and repair or replace the line when it is yours.

Where the utility ends and you begin

Pennsylvania American Water supplies Norristown, and generally the homeowner is responsible for the line from the connection at the main or the curb to the house, while the utility takes care of the main and the meter. The exact boundary can vary, so check with the utility if you are not sure.

If water is coming from the curb stop or the meter pit, it is usually the utility's concern. If it is coming from a wet yard patch between the curb and the house, it is probably yours.

Signs of a service line leak

Low water pressure through the house

A leak before the fixtures can reduce what reaches the house.

Meter runs with all taps off

Water is leaving the system somewhere on your side.

Wet or sunken ground between the street and the house

The line may be leaking into the soil.

Sudden rise in water bill

A steady leak at the line adds a lot of water use that you do not see.

Repair or replace the line

A short repair on a good line is the simplest answer. If the line is old galvanized steel, lead, or has failed more than once, replacement is the better long term answer.

Replacement can be done by open trench, or in some cases by methods that limit digging. We explain the route, the depth and what the surface will look like when we finish.

Service lines and Pennsylvania American Water

Pennsylvania American Water serves Norristown and has information on lead service lines and water quality on its website. If you are in an older home, ask the utility what your service line material is and what plans exist for it.

Before any digging, Pennsylvania requires a call to 811 so buried utilities are marked. We handle that step and plan the trench around marked lines.

According to PA One Call, anyone who plans to dig in Pennsylvania must call 811 first so buried utilities can be marked. We make that call before any excavation for a water line.

Digging, restoration and the water shutoff

We plan the trench to keep digging small, and we keep you informed about how long the water will be off. Lawns can be reseeded and hard surfaces repaired. We tell you what is included before we start.

If the leak is inside the house rather than under the yard, see leak detection for how we trace it.
