A sump pump pushes groundwater out of a pit in the basement floor. When it fails during heavy rain the basement can flood fast. The pump is a small machine with a short life and it gets used hardest when the weather is worst.

Why sump pumps fail

Common failures are a stuck float, a clogged intake screen, a burnt motor, a failed check valve, and a power outage. A pump may also be too small for the water it is asked to move.

A pump that cycles constantly is working hard. That can mean a high water table, a broken discharge line or a failing check valve that lets water flow back into the pit.

Water and basements in Norristown

Norristown sits on the Schuylkill River with Stony Creek and Saw Mill Run flowing into it. Low parts of the borough have seen flooding, including during Hurricane Ida in September 2021, when the U.S. Geological Survey documented flooding that covered much of the lower city.

A sump pump cannot stop river flooding, but it protects a basement from groundwater and heavy local rain. If your basement is in a flood prone area, ask us about backup power and discharge routing.

According to the National Weather Service, the Schuylkill River at Norristown reaches minor flood stage at 13 feet, moderate at 17 feet and major at 19 feet. Those are river stages, and a basement can still take on groundwater well below them.

Discharge, check valves and backup power

The discharge line should carry water away from the house to a place where it will not flow back. A check valve in that line stops water from running back into the pit after each cycle.

A battery backup pump runs when the power fails. We check that the battery is sized to run for the length of a storm. See basement waterproofing if the water keeps coming even when the pump is working.

Testing and maintaining your pump

Before the wet season, pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch. The float should rise, the pump should start, and the water should leave quickly. Listen for grinding. Check that the discharge pipe is not frozen or blocked and that it empties away from the foundation.

Clean the intake screen and the pit of silt and gravel. A pump that sits in sludge works harder and fails sooner. A quick test twice a year is cheap protection.

Discharge lines, freezing and winter

In winter the outside end of a discharge line can freeze solid, leaving the pump running with nowhere to send water. A freeze guard or a short open air gap at the outlet prevents this.

If water pools near the discharge and flows back toward the house, extend the line farther out. A pump that moves water in a circle is wasted work.
