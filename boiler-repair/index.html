Many older homes heat with a hot water boiler and radiators or baseboards. When it fails in winter the house cools fast, and the plumbing side of the system, including leaks and pressure, is something we repair.

We work on the water side of the boiler and the piping around it. For gas supply problems or combustion concerns we tell you plainly when to bring in a heating specialist.

Common boiler problems

Low pressure is a frequent complaint and usually means a leak or a failed expansion tank. A boiler that cycles on and off may have a sticking relief valve, a failed circulator or a dirty heat exchanger.

Banging or kettling noises point to scale or trapped air. Cold radiators often need bleeding or a zone valve repair.

Safety and gas

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. Do not turn on switches. Carbon monoxide is a danger in any home with fuel burning equipment, so keep working detectors on every floor.

We repair plumbing and water side faults. Combustion tuning and gas supply issues are a separate specialty and we say so when a call falls into that.

Boilers in older Norristown homes

Many older homes in the borough have boilers and radiators that have been running for decades. Original piping may be iron or steel and valves can be stuck from age.

If your system is old and failing, we talk through repair versus replacement. Replacement is a larger project and may involve a heating contractor.

According to the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, carbon monoxide alarms should be installed on every level of a home and outside sleeping areas. Any home with a boiler or other fuel burning appliance should have working alarms, and we suggest checking them each fall.

Repair or replace the boiler

A repair is worthwhile on a sound boiler. If the heat exchanger leaks or the unit is far past its useful life, replacement is more sensible.

Bleeding radiators and balancing a system

Air collects at the high points of a hot water system and stops radiators from heating at the top. Bleeding a radiator means opening a small valve until water, not air, comes out. It is a simple job, but if you have to do it often, the system is taking in air from somewhere and that source needs to be found.

Balancing is a related step. If rooms far from the boiler stay cold while near rooms overheat, valves may need adjusting, or a zone may have a failed valve. We trace the cause instead of just turning dials.

Winter planning for an older heating system

The best time to look at a boiler is before the first cold snap. Check the pressure gauge, look for drips around valves and the base, and listen for new noises. A small leak found in October is a cheap fix. The same leak found in January can leave a house without heat on the coldest night.

If you have an older boiler, keep a record of any repairs and the dates. That history helps us decide whether another repair makes sense or whether planning for replacement is wiser.
