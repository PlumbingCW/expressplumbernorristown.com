A water heater that leaks is the call we hear most often about hot water, and it is time sensitive. A weeping fitting is a small repair. A tank that has failed from the inside is not repairable and keeps pouring water until the supply is closed.

The first minutes decide how much damage you have. We walk you through the shutoff by phone, then send a plumber who can diagnose whether you need a part or a new heater.

Right now, before we arrive

Find the cold water supply valve on the pipe feeding the top of the heater and turn it clockwise to stop water entering the tank. If the heater is electric, switch off its breaker. If it is gas, turn the gas control to pilot or off and do not relight it if you smell gas. A gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.

If water is on the floor, keep it away from outlets and move stored items out of the puddle. A floor drain nearby will take most of it. Mop up what you can so the floor does not soak, and take photographs for your insurer before you clean everything.

What the symptoms usually point to

Water pooling around the base

A leak from a fitting or the relief valve is often repairable. A leak from the tank bottom usually means the tank has failed.

No hot water at all

On gas, a pilot or thermocouple problem. On electric, a tripped breaker, a high limit switch or a failed element.

Hot water runs out fast

A failed lower element, a sediment filled tank or a broken dip tube, all of which are repairable.

Rumbling or popping from the tank

Sediment hardened at the bottom. It can often be flushed, but a heavily scaled tank may be near the end of its life.

Gas, electric and hybrid units

Gas heaters rely on a burner, a pilot or igniter, a thermocouple and a flue that must draw properly. Electric heaters have two elements and thermostats in the tank wall. Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor and fan on top of an electric tank, and need the air space around them clear.

We repair all three types. If a hybrid unit is the problem, we check the condensate drain, the air filter and the error codes first because those are the common causes, and then move to the heat pump and the backup elements.

Water heaters in Norristown homes

Many homes in the borough keep the water heater in a basement, where a leak runs to the lowest point and can sit unnoticed. A small water alarm and a drain pan are cheap protection if yours is down there.

Water quality also affects tank life. Pennsylvania American Water, which supplies Norristown, publishes an annual water quality report at pennsylvaniaamwater.com/wqreports. If you want to know about your water, that is the source to read. A plumber can also check how much sediment comes out when the tank is flushed.

Model plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) require a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe on a tank water heater. We check both on every repair visit, following the manufacturer's installation guidelines.

What we do not repair

We do not repair a tank that is leaking from the shell. Water coming from the bottom of the tank or from the seam means the steel has corroded through, and a patch will not hold. We tell you early, because the right next step is a replacement.

When that happens, see our water heater replacement page for how a same day swap goes, or our tankless page if you are weighing a different type.

What affects the price of a repair

We quote the repair before starting it. These are the factors that change it.

The part that failed

A thermostat, igniter or element costs less than a gas valve or a hybrid compressor.

Age and model

Older units may need parts that are harder to find.

Location of the heater

A heater in a tight closet or a crawlspace takes longer to work on.

Time of the call

After hours visits may carry a different charge. We say so on the phone.
