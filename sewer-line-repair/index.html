A sewer line is the pipe that carries everything out of the house, and when it breaks the symptoms are hard to ignore. Repeated backups, wet spots in the yard, sewer smells and slow drains in more than one fixture all point to it.

The right repair depends on what the camera shows. A short broken section is different from a line that is crushed along most of its length. We explain what we see and the options that fit.

Confirm the break before you repair

We clear the line first and then run a camera. The video shows whether the pipe has a crack, an offset joint, a root mass or a collapse, and how far from the cleanout it is. This is the step that keeps you from paying to dig in the wrong place.

If the line is sound and only blocked, we clean it and tell you how to keep it that way. If it is failing, you get a plain description of the defect and the repair options.

Repair options for a damaged line

A spot repair replaces a short bad section. It means digging at one point, cutting out the broken pipe and joining new pipe. It is the lowest impact choice when only one area is bad.

A full replacement is considered when the pipe is bad along much of its length. Depending on the site, replacement can be by open trench or by a trenchless method. We explain what suits your yard, driveway and the depth of the pipe.

Sewer repair in an older borough

Norristown dates to 1812 and a lot of its housing is old. Houses of that era can still be connected with cast iron or clay laterals. The Norristown Municipal Waste Authority runs the public sewer, and your lateral to the connection is usually yours to maintain.

If a repair needs to cross a sidewalk or a street, the Municipality of Norristown may need to be involved. We tell you early whether the work you need might require municipal contact so it does not stall at the last minute.

Pipe repairs and replacements must meet the plumbing code that applies where you live, such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and work in a street or sidewalk is controlled by the municipality. Ask the Municipality of Norristown which permits apply before digging starts.

Digging, disruption and restoration

Digging is the part people worry about. We locate utilities first, choose the narrowest trench that works and keep the spoil on tarps. For a spot repair the trench is small and is often restored in the same visit or soon after.

If the camera shows the pipe is only blocked, see sewer line cleaning and hydro jetting before you decide on a dig.
