If water is rising in a tub when the washer drains, or a kitchen sink fills and will not empty, you have a blockage that is getting worse. Every extra load of water you send down the line has to go somewhere, and in a Norristown rowhouse or older twin that somewhere is often a basement floor drain or the lowest fixture in the house.

We clear drains at any hour. The first call sorts out whether you have a single fixture clog or a stoppage in the main line, because those need different tools and a different plan for the rest of the day.

What to do before the plumber gets there

Stop running water. That means no laundry, no dishwasher, and no flushing if more than one fixture is slow. A main line that is partly blocked will take a little water, but a full cycle from a washing machine can push it over the edge and into your floor drain.

If the backup is already at a floor drain or tub, move anything valuable off the floor and keep children and pets away from the standing water. Do not pour drain chemicals into a line that is backing up. They sit in the pipe, they can burn skin when a technician opens the trap, and they rarely clear what is actually causing the stoppage.

Signs the blockage is bigger than one fixture

More than one drain is slow

When a tub, a toilet and a basement floor drain all act up together, the stoppage is past the branch lines. That points to the main drain.

Gurgling when another fixture drains

Air is being pushed through water in a partly blocked line. Expect it to get worse, not better.

Water shows up in the lowest drain

A basement floor drain that bubbles or leaks after a shower upstairs is the classic main line warning.

Clog returns within days

A drain that clears and then slows again usually has buildup or roots further down the pipe than a store snake can reach.

A blocked drain gets worse with every flush

When snaking is not enough

A cable punches a hole through a clog. It does not always scrub the pipe wall, so grease and scale can leave a narrow channel that clogs again within weeks. If a line comes back after a clean, ask for a camera look before paying for the same clean twice. We cover that step on our sewer camera inspection page.

For stubborn grease or roots in a main line, hydro jetting washes the pipe wall with high pressure water. It costs more than a cable run, so we use it when the pipe condition and the history justify it, not as a default.

Drains in Norristown homes

Norristown is an old borough, incorporated in 1812, and many of its blocks were built long before modern plumbing standards. Older homes can have cast iron or clay lines, offsets at joints, and cleanouts that were buried under later additions, all of which change how a drain gets cleared.

The public sewer in the borough is run by the Norristown Municipal Waste Authority, and your responsibility usually runs from the house to the connection with that sewer. If the stoppage is out in the street, we will tell you that early so you can call the authority instead of paying for work that cannot fix it.

Model plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) call for cleanouts so that drains can be reached for cleaning. If your line has none, adding one is a sensible upgrade according to those standards and makes every future visit simpler.

Kitchen drains need different care than bathroom drains

Kitchen lines carry grease and food solids, so they collect a waxy buildup over time. Bathroom lines carry hair, soap and toilet paper. We pay attention to which kind of material comes out on the cable, because that tells you what to change at home.

A kitchen line that clogs every few months usually needs a periodic clean and a change in habits at the sink. A bathroom line that clogs once after a hair buildup may just need a strainer. We tell you which one you have rather than selling you a visit schedule you do not need.
