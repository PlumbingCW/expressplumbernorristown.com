A sewer camera is a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable. We feed it through a cleanout and watch the pipe on a monitor as it moves toward the street connection. It shows cracks, offsets, roots, scale and low spots without digging.

People usually call us for an inspection after a repeat clog, a slow main line, or before closing on a house, when they want to know what shape the buried line is in.

What the camera can and cannot tell you

The camera shows the inside of the pipe, the pipe material where it can be identified, and whether water stands in a low spot. It shows roots and where they enter, and breaks or offsets at joints.

It does not tell you the exact depth unless a locator is used, and it cannot see through a blockage that has not been cleared. If the line is full, we clear it first, then inspect.

Inspection before you buy a house

A home inspector looks at fixtures and visible plumbing. They cannot see the buried line. If the house is older, a camera view of the lateral is a reasonable thing to ask for before closing, because a broken or rooted lateral can be a repair of real size.

We give you a written summary you can show your agent or attorney, and we tell you honestly whether what we saw is normal wear or a problem.

Inspecting lines in an older borough

Norristown is county seat territory with a long history of development, and parts of the borough have been built up since the 1800s. Laterals of that age may be clay or cast iron and may have been patched over the decades.

Flooding along the Schuylkill River, including the high water from Hurricane Ida in 2021 that the U.S. Geological Survey documented across much of the lower city, can also leave lines with silt or displaced joints. A camera is a good way to check after a flood.

Municipal sewer crews often describe pipe defects using the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program (PACP) coding standard. We use plain language instead of codes, but the same categories of cracks, roots, offsets and deposits apply to your pipe.

Locating and marking the line

A camera tells you what is inside the pipe. A locator tells you where the pipe is. With a transmitter on the camera head we trace the line from above and mark the path and depth on the ground.

That information is useful before a patio, a fence or a tree planting. It also helps a contractor plan a repair so the dig is in the right place the first time.
