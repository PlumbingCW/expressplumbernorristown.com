Repiping means replacing the supply pipes that carry water through the house. It is a bigger project than a repair, and it is worth considering when the pipes are failing across the house instead of at a single spot.

We explain when repiping makes sense and when repair is enough, so you do not spend more than you need to.

When repiping makes sense

If you have had several leaks in a short time, or pinholes in copper that keep appearing in new places, the pipe wall is wearing out along its whole length. Another repair will not stop the next one.

Galvanized steel pipe corrodes from the inside, narrows over time and reduces pressure. Brown water at the tap and weak flow at fixtures far from the main often point to this. In that case repiping fixes the cause and not the symptom.

Signs your supply pipes are failing

Repeated pinhole leaks

New leaks at different spots show the pipe is thinning.

Brown or rusty water

Corrosion inside the pipe releases rust.

Low pressure at several fixtures

Mineral and rust buildup narrow the pipe.

Visible green or white crust on pipes

Corrosion on the outside as well as the inside.

How a repipe is done

We plan the route for new lines so that we open as little wall as possible. PEX is flexible and can be pulled through walls with fewer cuts than rigid pipe. We protect floors and furniture, cut access holes where needed and patch to a paint ready surface when the work is finished.

Water is off during the job, so we plan the work in stages. In a typical house the water can be restored at the end of each day when possible. We discuss this plan before we begin.

Repiping older Norristown houses

Norristown has a lot of housing from the early and mid twentieth century and earlier. Houses of that age may have galvanized steel supply lines or old copper. If you are buying one, a plumbing inspection is a good idea.

The Municipality of Norristown handles permits in the borough. Whether repiping needs one is a question to put to the municipality, and we help gather the details they ask for.

Model plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) list the approved materials for water distribution, including copper, PEX and CPVC. We choose among them based on your house and follow the manufacturer's installation guidelines.

Repipe or keep repairing

A single leak on otherwise sound pipe is a repair. If you keep paying for leaks, the cost of repairs adds up, and each one is a risk of water damage. We give you an honest comparison.

If a leak is hidden, see leak detection. If the leak was a burst, see burst pipe repair. For the line that comes from the street, see water service line repair.

Water quality and what we can tell you

Brown water that clears after running is often rust from galvanized lines. Water that smells or tastes metallic may be from corroded pipe or from the source. Pennsylvania American Water publishes an annual water quality report at pennsylvaniaamwater.com/wqreports, and a lab test of your tap tells you more about what is coming out of your pipes.

We do not claim that new pipes will change taste or hardness. They will remove rust and restore flow, and a lab test is the way to see anything beyond that.

Living in the house during the work

Most repipes can be done while you live in the house. We stage the work, restore water at the end of each day when possible, and protect floors and furniture. You may have a few hours without water on some days, and we tell you when.

We also tell you what to move out of the way and where holes will be cut. Patching and painting are discussed before we begin, so you are not surprised by a finish that needs a separate trade.
