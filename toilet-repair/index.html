Common toilet failures and what causes them

Most running toilets have a worn flapper, a fill valve that no longer shuts off, or a chain that is too short or tangled. These are small parts and quick repairs.

A rocking toilet or a leak at the base usually means the wax ring or the bolts need attention. We reseat the toilet with a new seal and check the floor flange for damage.

Toilets in Norristown homes

Older bathrooms in the borough often have toilets that predate modern low flow designs, with narrower trapways and worn floor flanges. A toilet set on a cracked flange will keep rocking no matter how many times you reset it.

We check the flange and the floor under the toilet when we reset it. If the floor has soft spots from earlier leaks, we tell you before we put a new toilet on top of a problem.

According to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's WaterSense program, toilets that earn the label use no more than 1.28 gallons per flush and are tested for flush performance. That is the standard we point people to when choosing a new toilet.

Repair or replace a toilet

If the toilet is sound and a part has failed, repair is the answer. If the bowl is cracked, the toilet is repeatedly clogging or the flush performance is poor, a new toilet pays for itself in water and aggravation.

We install new toilets and remove the old one. If the bathroom is being remodeled, we coordinate with your tile and floor schedule. See also faucet repair if the sink is next on your list.

Low flow, water savings and flush quality

A toilet that wastes water through a silent leak can add a lot to a bill. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing, and look in the bowl. If color appears, the flapper is leaking.

If you are choosing a new toilet, the flush quality matters more than the water rating alone. We install models that clear well and tell you which ones to avoid.
