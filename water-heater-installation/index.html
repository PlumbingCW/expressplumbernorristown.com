Sizing a heater to your household

The right size depends on how many people use hot water at the same time. A family of five with two showers running in the morning needs more capacity than a couple. A tank that is too small gives out in the middle of a shower, and one that is too large wastes energy.

We ask about the number of bathrooms, the biggest hot water uses and whether you have a soaking tub. We then suggest a size and a type. If you prefer a different size we explain the trade off.

What a clean installation includes

A good installation is not only the heater. It is a drain pan where a leak would damage a floor, a discharge pipe from the relief valve routed to a safe place, shutoff valves and flexible connectors that can be serviced, and an expansion tank where the system needs one.

We also label the shutoffs so you know where to turn off water and power. That small step saves hours the day something fails.

Installing in Norristown homes

Basements and utility closets in the borough can be tight. We check clearances and the route for the old and new heater before we schedule. If you rent, we coordinate with the property manager when the building has shared utilities.

Permit requirements for a new installation are set by the Municipality of Norristown. Contact the municipality or ask us, and we will help gather what is needed.

We follow the manufacturer's installation guidelines for clearances, venting and clearances to combustibles. Model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) also set rules for relief valve discharge piping and expansion control.

Venting, gas lines and combustion air

Gas water heaters need a vent that carries exhaust out of the house and enough air to burn properly. A vent that slopes the wrong way, has a loose joint or is too small can let exhaust back into the room. We inspect and correct these items as part of an installation instead of connecting a new heater to a vent we have not looked at.

The gas line also matters. If the pipe is too small for a larger heater, the burner will not get the gas it needs. We check the line size and the shutoff valve and tell you what is needed.

Warranty, registration and what to keep

Keep the heater's model and serial number, the installation date and your receipt. Registering the heater with the maker is usually what activates a longer warranty. We write the details on the unit so you can find them later.

We also show you the temperature setting and the relief valve, and tell you what a normal noise sounds like. A homeowner who knows the heater is better placed to notice trouble early.
