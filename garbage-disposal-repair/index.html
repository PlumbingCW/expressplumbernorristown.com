A garbage disposal sits at the center of a kitchen sink. When it jams, leaks or sends water back up the other side of a double sink, the whole kitchen slows down.

We repair disposals when repair is sensible and replace them when it is not.

Safety first: never put a hand in the disposal

Turn off the wall switch and, if you can reach it, unplug the unit or turn off the breaker. Never put your hand into the disposal, even if it is off. If something is stuck, use tongs or pliers.

If the unit hums but does not spin, it is jammed. Many disposals have a hex socket on the bottom for a wrench and a reset button, and a quick reset can free a jam. If it will not respond, call us.

What the symptoms usually mean

Hums but does not spin

A jam. Use the hex socket and the reset button, or call us.

Leaks under the unit

A failed seal or a loose flange. Often means replacement.

Water backs up in the other sink

The drain line past the disposal is blocked. See drain cleaning.

Smells even after cleaning

Buildup in the grind chamber or the drain line.

What a disposal should and should not handle

A disposal is built for small food scraps and plenty of cold water. Grease, bones, coffee grounds in quantity, pasta, rice and fibrous vegetables like celery can build up in the line and cause clogs.

Most disposal problems are really drain line problems. If the clog is past the disposal, a cable through the trap clears it. We check the line before we blame the unit.

Disposals in Norristown kitchens

Older rowhouses and twins in the borough often have kitchen drains with a long horizontal run to the stack. Grease from the sink settles in that run and eventually clogs the line behind the disposal.

If your disposal is fine but the sink drains slowly, the problem is likely in the pipe, not the unit. We clear the line, and a periodic cleaning may keep the kitchen running.

The National Electrical Code (NEC) governs how a disposal is wired, including grounding and the switch or outlet that controls it. We stop at plumbing and bring in a licensed electrician for wiring beyond a simple connection.

Installing a new disposal

We install new disposals, sized to your household and the sink. We also install the electrical switch if one is missing, with the work done safely. For wiring beyond a simple connection we may bring in a licensed electrician.

Daily habits that keep a disposal healthy

Run cold water before, during and for a few seconds after grinding. Feed scraps in small amounts. Keep grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, eggshell piles, pasta and rice out. These do not grind well and settle in the drain line.

Grinding a few ice cubes and a slice of citrus now and then freshens the chamber and scrubs the walls. Chemical cleaners are not needed and can damage seals.

Dishwashers and the disposal connection

The dishwasher drain connects to the disposal inlet in many kitchens. If the dishwasher leaves standing water, the disposal knockout plug may never have been removed after a new install, or the connection may be clogged.

We check that connection when we work on a disposal, because it is a common cause of a dishwasher that smells or does not drain.
