A leak you cannot see does not wait. Water behind a wall softens drywall, a leak under a slab can wash soil from beneath a floor, and a buried supply line can lose water for weeks before anyone notices. The first clue is often the bill or a sound in the pipes.

We find leaks with listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure tests, then tell you where the water is going and what repair fits. Most of the time that means opening a small section, not a whole wall.

First steps when you suspect a leak

Check the water meter. Turn off every tap and appliance, then look at the meter. If the dial or the small triangle keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere after the meter. If it is still, the problem may be a fixture that runs only when used.

If you see active water, shut off the main valve and the water heater. Photograph the damage for your insurer, and call us. Do not cut into the wall yourself, because a small hole in the wrong place can cause more damage than the leak.

Signs of a hidden leak

Water bill jumps without a reason

A steady rise with no change in use often means a slow leak on the supply side.

Sound of running water

A hiss or trickle with every fixture off is a strong clue.

Warm spot on a floor

Hot supply lines under a slab can make part of a floor warm.

Stains, bubbling paint or musty smell

Moisture is traveling through a wall or ceiling from a source above or behind.

How we trace the leak

We start by isolating the system. We close valves to see which section holds pressure and which loses it. That narrows a house down to a line, a zone or an appliance supply.

We then use acoustic listening gear, a thermal camera and moisture meters to pinpoint the spot. Where a line runs through a slab or underground we can trace its path. The goal is a small, accurate opening, not a guess followed by a large one.

Leaks and water service in Norristown

Public water in the borough is supplied by Pennsylvania American Water. The meter and the line from the street to the house sit at the boundary of what the utility and the homeowner own, so a leak before the meter and a leak after it are handled differently.

If your meter keeps moving after every fixture is off, the leak is on your side. We trace it. If the water appears at the curb or street, the utility should be called. We help you decide which, and you can also confirm details on your Pennsylvania American Water bill.

According to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's WaterSense program, the average household's leaks can waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water each year. A running meter with everything off is the quickest way to tell whether you are one of them.

What happens after the leak is found

We explain the cause and the repair options, with a clear price before work starts. Some leaks need only a small patch, some need a section of pipe replaced, and a few lines are so worn that repiping is the sounder choice than a string of repairs.

If the leak is on the buried line from the street, our water service line repair page explains your options. We also tell you what to photograph and what to keep for an insurance claim.
