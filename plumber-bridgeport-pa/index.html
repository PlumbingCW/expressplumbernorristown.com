What older Bridgeport housing means for your pipes

Bridgeport grew around the river and the railroad, and its history includes paper, flour, cotton and woolen mills, a steel works and brickyards. The housing that grew up around those industries is mostly old, and old houses carry old plumbing.

In a house of that age the supply lines, drains and shutoff valves may be original, and some of them may no longer work as they should. When a leak call comes in, we look at the whole run and not only at the spot that is dripping.

Rowhouses and shared walls

Rowhouses share walls and sometimes share drain lines. A blockage in a shared line can show up in a neighbor's basement first. We ask whether neighbors have the same symptoms, because it tells us whether to look at your lateral or a shared run.

If you rent, tell your landlord and ask for access to the basement. If you own, keep the cleanout clear of boxes so a cable can reach the line.

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, Bridgeport covers well under one square mile. That density is why a single leak can affect several neighboring houses, and why we ask about neighbors when we take the call.

Services we bring to ZIP 19405

Most Bridgeport calls fall into a few kinds. Drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning for basement backups come first in the 19405 ZIP, followed by leak detection for pipes behind walls and burst pipe repair in winter.

Water heater failures are common in older basements, so we also handle water heater replacement across Bridgeport. For planned work like a bathroom update, see plumbing installation.

Winter freezes in rowhouse basements

Rowhouse basements often have supply lines along exterior walls and at the front of the house where the line enters. In a hard freeze those pipes are the first to go. Letting a faucet drip, opening cabinet doors and keeping the basement above freezing are simple steps.

If a pipe has frozen but not burst, do not use an open flame. Warm it slowly and call us if it does not thaw. See burst pipe repair for what to do if it has already split.

Working near the river

Streets close to the Schuylkill can see water in the basement during heavy storms. A sump pump with a battery backup is the best defense for a low basement. If you have one, test it before the storm season.

When river levels are high, we tell you what a pump can and cannot do. A pump handles groundwater, not a river that has left its banks.
