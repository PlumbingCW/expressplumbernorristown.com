Central Norristown is the borough's downtown. It holds the county courthouse, the shops and offices along Main Street and the homes on the blocks around them. The National Register of Historic Places lists the area as the Central Norristown Historic District. This page is for the owners, managers and residents of that mix of buildings.

For the whole borough, see our Norristown plumbing page. Homes on the west side of town are covered on the West Norristown page. Across the Schuylkill River, Bridgeport has its own page.

A district where business and homes share the blocks

The National Register describes the Central Norristown Historic District as the central business district and the residential areas around it, with workers' housing and larger dwellings. It covers about 245 acres and roughly 1,900 buildings. The buildings named in the listing include the Montgomery County Courthouse, the Philadelphia and Western Railroad Station of 1931, the Scheidt Brewery, the county jail, the Y.M.C.A. and the Odd Fellows Hall.

For a plumber, the useful point is the mix. A shop with a restroom and a prep sink has different needs from a family house two streets away. We ask what kind of building it is, who is responsible for it and how soon the water has to be back on.

Streets and landmarks in Central Norristown

Main Street, also called Ridge Pike, runs east and west through downtown. The Norristown Transportation Center is the borough's main transit hub, and the county courthouse stands in the district. The listing describes the district as roughly bounded by Stoney Creek and by Walnut, Lafayette and Fornace Streets, and it was added to the register on November 23, 1984. Sources: the National Register listing and Wikipedia's article on Norristown.

These landmarks help you place us on the map. They do not tell us what any building's plumbing looks like, and we do not claim that they do. If you manage a building here, tell us the address and we will plan from what we find.

Services for downtown buildings

Shops and offices often start with commercial plumbing, which covers restrooms, break room sinks, floor drains and water heaters. A restaurant or a shop with a grease or sink blockage may need hydro jetting after a basic cable clear has failed.

Residents on the surrounding blocks call about the usual set: water heater repair, leak detection and drain cleaning. When a pipe bursts in a building that has other tenants, our burst pipe repair page explains the first steps.

What we could not confirm

We could not confirm the pipe materials, the age of the plumbing or the drain layout of any building in the district. We also could not confirm which buildings are inside the boundary, since the listing gives general limits. A landlord or building manager is the right person to ask about shared shutoffs.

If you rent, tell your landlord first. If you own or manage the building, call us with the address and we start from there.

Before you call about a downtown leak or backup

These steps help in a shop, an office or a house.

Find the shutoff

Locate the main valve for your space. In a building with several tenants, ask the manager which valve serves you.

Protect what is below

Move stock, files and electronics away from the water if you can do it safely.

Note the symptoms

Tell us which fixtures are affected and whether neighbors in the building see the same thing.
