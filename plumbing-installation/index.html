Installing new plumbing is different from repairing old plumbing. It means planning where lines will run, sizing them properly, and coordinating with the other trades who work on a remodel.

We help homeowners, landlords and contractors with new plumbing installs of all sizes.

What we install

We install toilets, sinks, faucets, showers and tubs, laundry hookups, outdoor spigots, dishwasher and ice maker connections, water softener connections, and new supply or drain lines for additions and remodels.

We also install and test backflow prevention devices where water must be protected from contamination, such as on irrigation systems and some commercial lines.

Backflow prevention and testing

A backflow preventer stops water from flowing backward into the clean water supply. They are used on irrigation lines, in some commercial buildings, and wherever a cross connection could contaminate the supply.

Many devices must be tested on a schedule, and testing is done by certified testers. We install the device and tell you what testing your situation calls for. Your water utility or the Municipality of Norristown can confirm requirements.

Remodel plumbing in Norristown

Remodeling an older home in the borough often turns up surprises, such as old galvanized lines, cast iron drains and shutoff valves that no longer work. We plan for that by checking the existing system before we quote.

The Municipality of Norristown sets permit rules for plumbing work. Ask the municipality which apply to your project and we will help provide what they ask for.

New plumbing work has to meet the plumbing code that applies locally, and the model International Plumbing Code (IPC) is the starting point many jurisdictions use. We install to those standards and tell you which inspections the Municipality of Norristown may require.

Choosing fixtures that last

The fixture you pick matters as much as the install. A toilet with a larger trapway clogs less often, a faucet with a ceramic cartridge lasts longer than one with rubber washers, and a shower valve with a replaceable cartridge can be serviced without opening the wall. We tell you which models give you the fewest callbacks.

If you are not sure what to buy, we can give you two or three options that fit your budget and your plumbing. Buying fixtures before the rough in is finished also lets us confirm they fit the rough in dimensions, which prevents a surprise on the day.

Planning for the next repair

Every installation is a chance to make the next repair easier. We add shutoff valves at each fixture, label main shutoffs, and leave access panels where a valve or cleanout would otherwise be buried behind a finished wall.

Those small choices turn a future emergency into a ten minute fix. They cost little during a remodel and are expensive to add afterward.
