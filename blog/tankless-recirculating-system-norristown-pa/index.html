Tankless Recirculating System for Norristown, PA Homes

Tankless heaters make hot water fast, but the wait at a far tap can still be long. A recirculation setup can shorten it. Here is how.

The wait for hot water at a tap far from the heater is caused by cool water sitting in the pipe. A tankless unit does not remove that wait. It only makes hot water quickly once water flows through it.

A recirculating system moves hot water through the pipes so that it is near the tap when you want it.

How recirculation works

A small pump moves water from the hot line through a return path back to the heater. This keeps hot water close to the fixtures. The pump can run all the time, on a timer, or by a button or sensor.

Some tankless units have a recirculation feature built in. Others need an add on pump. Houses with a dedicated return line are simplest. Houses without one may use a pump under the far sink that uses the cold line as the return.

Types of systems

A dedicated return line loops hot water back to the heater. It is the most efficient, but it needs a pipe run that older houses may not have.

A comfort pump under the sink uses the cold line as the return. It is simpler to install, and it makes the cold tap warm for a short time. Both approaches can be put on a timer or demand control.

The energy trade off

Hot water moving in pipes loses heat, and the heater has to make it up. Running a recirculation loop all day wastes energy. A timer or a demand button keeps the benefit and cuts the cost.

Insulating pipes helps as well. If your tankless heater is failing or needs maintenance, see tankless water heater for service, and water heater installation if you are replacing a tank.

What to check before adding a pump

Not every tankless unit works well with a recirculation pump. Some units have a minimum flow and may shut off if the pump moves too little water. Others have a built in buffer tank for recirculation. Check the manual or ask us before you add a pump.

Also check the pipe condition. Recirculation moves water through pipes more often, and weak spots show up sooner. If the pipes are old and thin, plan for that.

Controls that save energy

Timers run the pump at the hours you use hot water. A push button starts it on demand. Smart controls learn your habits. The point is the same, which is to run the pump only when it is useful.

Insulating the hot pipes between the heater and the far fixture reduces heat loss and shortens the wait even without a pump. It is the first thing we suggest if the run is long.

What to expect during an install

An under sink pump install means connecting the pump to the hot and cold lines at the far fixture, plugging it in and setting the control. A dedicated return line is a larger project that opens walls or ceilings.

We explain which approach fits your house before we quote. For a long wait in an older house, the simple pump often gives most of the benefit.

Is the wait really the pipe

Before you add anything, time how long hot water takes to reach the far tap. If the heater is far from the fixture and the pipe is large, the wait is natural. If hot water takes much longer than expected, the heater or a valve may be the issue.

We check flow and temperature during a visit and tell you whether recirculation, insulation or a repair is the right answer.

We take these calls in Blue Bell as well, where larger homes often have longer pipe runs.

Water saved while you wait

Every gallon that runs down the drain while you wait for hot water is water you paid for twice, once to the utility and once as sewer service. A household that runs a tap for a minute several times a day can send a surprising amount of water to the drain over a year.

A recirculation setup turns that waste into a small amount of electricity. For some homes that is a good trade, and for others the wait is short enough that it is not worth the cost. We help you decide with the real layout in front of us.

Notes for Norristown houses

Many older Norristown houses were not built with return lines, and the hot supply may run a long way from a basement heater to a second floor bath. An under sink pump is often the practical solution.

Check pressure, because recirculation can reveal weaknesses in old pipe. Homes in Bridgeport often have tight layouts where the run is short, so the wait may be less.

Manufacturer guidelines decide whether a given tankless model can run with a recirculation pump, and some units need a built in buffer tank. Check the installation manual before you add any pump.
