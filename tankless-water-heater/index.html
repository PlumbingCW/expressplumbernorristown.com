A tankless water heater heats water as it flows instead of keeping a tank hot. That means hot water that does not run out in a long shower, and a unit small enough to hang on a wall.

It also means a bigger installation job, a gas line or electrical service sized for the unit, and a yearly maintenance step that tank owners never worry about.

Is tankless right for your house

Tankless makes sense for homes where the tank heater is taking up space you want, where hot water use is high, or where the old heater is failing and you are open to the upgrade. It is less compelling for a small household that uses little hot water, or a house where the gas line and venting would need major work.

We look at gas line size, venting route, water pressure, and the number of fixtures that run at once. We then tell you whether the job is simple or involved, and whether the benefits justify it for your home.

Why tankless units need flushing

Scale builds on the heat exchanger

Minerals in the water deposit on the heat exchanger and reduce flow and efficiency.

Error codes appear

Many scale related faults show on the display before the unit shuts down.

Water turns lukewarm

A scaled exchanger cannot heat as much water as it should.

Fluctuating temperature

Partly blocked passages cause swings during a shower.

How we flush a tankless unit

We close the water and gas, attach hoses to the service valves and circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for a set time. We then flush with clean water, clean the inlet screen, and put the unit back into service.

Service valves make this a simple job. If your unit was installed without them we can add them. A yearly flush is the most useful single thing you can do to extend the life of the unit.

Tankless in Norristown homes

For people in the borough, the question is often space and the gas line. Basements and utility closets can be small, and the vent route for a tankless unit must go somewhere safe. We check this before we recommend an install.

Pennsylvania American Water supplies the borough and publishes its water quality report at pennsylvaniaamwater.com/wqreports. If you are concerned about scale, that report and a test of your own tap are the best places to start. We do not quote a hardness figure from memory.

Tankless manufacturers publish installation guidelines that set the gas line size, the vent materials and lengths, and the clearances around the unit. We follow those standards because a vent that is wrong is a safety problem and can void a warranty.

Repair, replacement and recirculation

Tankless repairs usually fall into scale, a failed flow sensor, an igniter, a fan or a venting problem. If the unit is a good one and the repair is modest we fix it. If it is badly worn we tell you.

If you are deciding between a tankless and a conventional heater after a failure, see water heater replacement. If you want a faster hot water at distant fixtures, ask us about a recirculation setup.
